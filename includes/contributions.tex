\section[Koszul complexes and relative homological algebra]{\citefield{CGRST2024}{title}}

In this paper, we study persistence modules over a finite poset $(I, \mathord{\leq})$ using the functorial perspective (\cref{SS:functor}). We extend the results of \cref{S:persistent-homological-algebra} to the \index{relative homological algebra}\textbf{relative homological case}. Instead of approximating functors over $I$ by projective resolutions, where each term is a sum of free functors, we approximate them by other ``simple'' functors. Given a collection $\cc{P}$ of such simple functors, we define $\cc{P}$-exactness, $\cc{P}$-projectives, $\cc{P}$-covers, and $\cc{P}$-resolutions, which are the relative versions of the ``absolute'' notions defined in \cref{S:persistent-homological-algebra}. A \index{free functor!relative ($\symcal {P}$-)}\textbf{$\cc{P}$-free functor} is a functor that is isomorphic to a direct sum of elements of $\cc{P}$. The collection $\cc{P}$ is then \index{acyclic (collection of functors)}\textbf{acyclic} if every $\cc{P}$-projective is $\cc{P}$-free. Under this condition, we can then define \index{Betti diagram!relative ($\symcal {P}$-)}\textbf{Betti diagrams relative to $\cc{P}$}: given a functor $M \colon I \to \Vect$ and an element $a \in J$ such that $\cc{T}(a) \neq 0$, we denote by $\beta_{\cc{P}}^d M(a)$ the multiplicity of $\cc{T}(a)$ in the $d^{\th}$ term of the minimal $\cc{P}$-projective resolution of $M$.

The principal result of this paper is that, under reasonable conditions on $\cc{P}$, we can compute the relative Betti diagrams using local Koszul complexes. First, assume that the collection $\cc{P}$ is parameterized by a functor $\cc{T} \colon (J, \mathord{\preceq})^{\op} \to \Fun(I, \Vect)$ where $(J, \mathord{\preceq})$ is a finite upper semilattice (see Paper \ref{PC:CGRST2024}, \textsection 3.6) and $\cc{P} = \{\cc{T}(a) \mid \cc{T}(a) \neq 0, \; a \in J\}$. The parameterization functor $\cc{T}$ is \index{parameterization functor!thin}\textbf{thin} if, for all $a \preceq b$ in $J$, the space of natural transformation $\Nat(\cc{T}(b), \cc{T}(a))$ is of dimension at most $1$. In this case, the collection $\cc{P}$ is acyclic, and so we can talk about Betti diagrams relative to $\cc{P}$.
Given a functor $M \colon I \to \Vect$, we define the functor indexed by $J$
\[
  \cc{R} M \colon \left\{ \begin{array}{ccc}
    J & \to & \Vect \\
    a & \mapsto & \Nat(\cc{T}(a), M).
  \end{array} \right.
\]
The main result is the following; compare with \cref{!T:Koszul-computes-Betti}.

\begin{theorem}[{Paper \ref{PC:CGRST2024}, Corollary 5.20}]
  Let $(J, \mathord{\preceq})$ be an upper semilattice and $\cc{T}$ a thin parameterization functor. Assume that
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item the set $\{a \in J \mid \cc{T}(a) = 0\}$ is closed under joins, and
    \item for all $a, b$ in $J$, if $b$ is minimal $\succeq a$ such that $\Nat(\cc{T}(b), \cc{T}(a)) = 0$, then $\cc{T}(a) = 0$.
  \end{enumerate}
  Then, for all functors $M \colon I \to \Vect$, all $a$ in $J$ such that $\cc{T}(a) \neq 0$, and all $d \geq 0$,
  \[
    \beta_{\cc{P}}^d M(a) = \dim H_d(\cc{K}_a \cc{R} M).
  \]
\end{theorem}

In other words, under the listed conditions, we can compute the Betti diagrams relative to $\cc{P}$ using the standard Koszul complexes defined on $\cc{R} M \colon J \to \Vect$.

Paper \ref{PC:CGRST2024} ends with an extensive list of examples of collection $\cc{P}$ and parameterization functors $\cc{T}$ where the conditions of the theorem hold. This includes examples that have been studied in previous works, including \textbf{rectangles}, \textbf{lower hooks} \cite{BOO2025}, and \textbf{single-source spread modules} \cite{BBH2024}. In fact, this work was in part motivated by the study of exact structures for persistence modules, in particular relative to lower hooks, in \cite[Section 4]{BOO2025}. We have also implemented the computation of Betti diagrams relative to lower hooks in Python \cite{lowerhooks}: see \cref{F:lowerhooks} for a sample output.

\subsection*{Erratum}

Thank you to Tada Shunsuke for pointing out the following mistake.

In \textsection 4.4, we write that a functor $F \colon J \to \Vect$ is isomorphic to a subfunctor of $\K_J$ if, and only if, $F$ is a filtration and $\dim F(a) \leq 1$ for every $a$ in $J$. This does not hold in all generality: consider the functor $F$ defined by
\[
  \begin{tikzcd}
    \K
      \arrow[r, "1"]
      \arrow[d, "1" swap]
    & \K
    \\
    \K
    & \K
      \arrow[l, "1"]
      \arrow[u, "\lambda" swap]
  \end{tikzcd}
\]
where $\lambda \in \K \setminus \{0, 1\}$. Then $F$ is a filtration with pointwise dimension at most $1$, but is not isomorphic to a subfunctor of $\K_J$. As a correction, we restrict the claim to the case where $J$ is an upper semilattice. There is no impact on the rest of the paper.

\section[Algebraic Wasserstein distances]{\citefield{AGRS2025}{title}}

In this paper, we define algebraic $p$-Wasserstein distances for tame persistence modules, proving that the sequence of collections $\cc{S} = \{\cc{S}_\varepsilon\}_{\varepsilon \geq 0}$ of \cref{SS:algebraic-wasserstein} is indeed a noise system. This is an alternate proof to that of \cite{SkrabaTurner2023}.

The difficult noise system axiom to prove is the exact sequence one (\cref{!D:noise-system}), and specifically the following result (a simplified version of [Paper \ref{PC:AGRS2025}, Lemma 4.15]):

\begin{proposition}
\label{P:triangular-ineq}
  Let $0 \to U \xrightarrow{f} V \xrightarrow{g} W \to 0$ be an exact sequence of tame persistence modules, and $p \in [1, \infty]$ a number. Then
  \[
    \| V \|_p \leq \| U \|_p + \| W \|_p.
  \]
\end{proposition}

Our proof strategy is to observe that the morphism $f$ is a monomorphism and that $W \cong \coker f$, and then study cases where the $p$-norm of $\coker f$ is easy to compute. We identify the following notion.

\begin{definition}
\label{!D:bar-to-bar}
  A morphism $f \colon V \to W$ of tame persistence modules is \index{bar-to-bar morphism}\textbf{bar-to-bar} when there exists a matrix representation of $f$ such that each row and column has at most one nonzero coefficient.
\end{definition}

In other words, for a bar-to-bar morphism, there exist barcode decompositions of the domain and codomain such that each bar of the domain maps to at most one bar of the codomain. The key result is the following.

\begin{theorem}[{Paper \ref{PC:AGRS2025}, Theorem 3.14}]
\label{T:induced-btb}
  Let $f \colon V \hookrightarrow W$ be a monomorphism of tame persistence modules. Then there exists a bar-to-bar monomorphism $f_b \colon V \hookrightarrow W$ such that $\| \coker f_b \|_p \leq \| \coker f \|_p$ for all $p \in [1, \infty]$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch of \cref{P:triangular-ineq}]
  If $f \colon U \hookrightarrow V$ is bar-to-bar, then we know the barcode of $W \cong \coker f$ from the barcodes of $U$ and $V$, and the inequality follows from the triangular inequality for usual $p$-norms.
  
  For arbitrary $f \colon U \hookrightarrow V$, let $f_b \colon U \hookrightarrow W$ be a bar-to-bar morphism as in \cref{T:induced-btb}. Then
  \[
    \| V \|_p \leq \| U \|_p + \| \coker f_b \|_p \leq \| U \|_p + \| W \|_p.
  \]
  The first inequality comes from the previous observation, and the second from \cref{T:induced-btb}.
\end{proof}

In this paper, we also introduce additional parameterizations on $p$-Wasserstein distances. First, in the definition of $\varepsilon$-closeness, we can replace the sum in \eqref{Eq:epsilon-close} with any $q$-norm, $q \in [1, \infty]$:
\[
  \| \alpha(\ker f), \alpha(\coker f), \alpha(\ker g), \alpha(\coker g) \|_q \leq \varepsilon.
\]
Moreover, when taking the $p$-norm of a persistence module $V \cong \bigoplus_\alpha \K_{S_\alpha}$, we can apply a nondecreasing function $T \colon \R \to \R$ to intervals $S_\alpha$ before computing their length:
\[
  \| V \|_p \coloneq \| (T(t(S_\alpha)) - T(s(S_\alpha))) \|_p.
\]
This allows us to weight the importance of bars at different points of the indexing set $\R$. Given these parameterized pseudometrics, we are able to explicitly compute the \index{hierarchical stabilization}\textbf{hierarchical stabilization} of persistence modules, which are real-valued functions that we can feed into machine learning frameworks \cite{GafvertChacholski2021,SCLRÖ2017}. In the paper, we apply this analysis pipeline to classification tasks for a synthetic dataset, as well as a dataset of brain vessel models of patients of various ages. The classifying model learns the optimal parameters $p$ and $T$ in order to, for example, classify patients by age based on their brain vessel structure. The parameter $q$ is fixed equal to $1$, the standard value, and $T$ is the cumulative density of a mixed Gaussian model. The key observation is that, when $p$ is large, then the model focuses on long bars, and when $p$ is close to $1$, then it focuses on short bars. It had been observed that short bars are more relevant for this dataset \cite{BMMPS2016}, and we recover this result.

\section[Barcode matchings and \texorpdfstring{$p$}{p}-norms]{\citefield{GRRS2026}{title}}

In this paper, we refine the tools introduced in Paper \ref{PC:AGRS2025}. In particular, we extend the notion of bar-to-bar morphisms (\cref{!D:bar-to-bar}) to pointwise finite-dimensional persistence modules and prove analogous results to those of Paper \ref{PC:AGRS2025}. In particular, we develop a simpler method of constructing bar-to-bar morphisms from mono- and epimorphisms. We present the results here for tame persistence modules; see Paper \ref{PC:GRRS2026} for the full results for p.f.d.\ modules.

\begin{definition}
  Let $V \cong \bigoplus_j \K_{[a_j, b_j)}$ and $W \cong \bigoplus_i \K_{[c_i, d_i)}$ be tame persistence modules with fixed persistence bases. For every morphism $f \colon V \to W$, we define $\Delta f \colon V \to W$ as the projection of $f$ along the map of vector spaces
  \begin{align*}
    \hom(X, Y) & \cong \bigoplus_{i, j} \hom(\K_{[a_j, b_j)}, \K_{[c_i, d_i)}) \\
    & \to \bigoplus_{\substack{i, j \\ b_j = d_i}} \hom(\K_{[a_j, b_j)}, \K_{[c_i, d_i)}) \\
    & \hookrightarrow \hom(X, Y).
  \end{align*}
  In other words, considering a matrix representation of $f$, we only keep the nonzero coefficients whose row and column are labeled by bars with the same end-point.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[{\cref{C!T:Delta-is-btb}}]
  For every morphism $f$ in $\Tame$, the morphism $\Delta f$ is bar-to-bar.
\end{theorem}

We also prove that $p$-Wasserstein distances are pseudometrics for p.f.d.\ persistence modules using the noise system approach. Our approach to proving the inequality of $p$-norms of cokernels uses the integral of the rank function. In particular, by \cref{C!P:p-norm-by-integral}, for all $p \in (1, \infty)$,
\[
  \| V \|_p^p = \int_{\Omega(\R)} p (p - 1) (t - s)^{p - 2} \rank V(s \leq t) \text{d}s \text{d}t.
\]

In this more general setting, we also prove the isometry theorem between the algebraic and combinatorial Wasserstein distances.

\begin{theorem}[{\cref{C!T:isometry}}]
  For all $p \in [1, \infty]$ and all $V$ and $W$ in $\Pfd$, we have
  \[
    d_{\cc{S}^p}(V, W) = d_p(\barcode(V), \barcode(W)).
  \]
\end{theorem}

In addition to this new proof of the isometry result, we also study the properties of matchings induced by morphisms. Note that a bar-to-bar morphism can be seen as an algebraic representation of a barcode matching: by \cref{!D:bar-to-bar}, such a morphism has a matrix representation with at most one nonzero coefficient in each row and column. The set of coordinates of nonzero coefficients defines a matching between the persistence bases, and therefore the barcodes, of the domain and codomain. We thus define matchings induced by morphisms. Building on the results of \cite{BauerLesnick2015,GST2025}, we show that our matchings are \textbf{additive} but not \textbf{functorial} (\cref{C!D:matching-operator}). In fact, we show that an operator that associates to each morphism a matching cannot be both additive and functorial (\cref{C!P:split-nand-functorial}).

Using the strategy of \cite{BauerLesnick2015}, we define the induced matching of a morphism $f \colon V \to W$ by taking an epi-mono factorization $f = m \circ e$, inducing matchings on $m$ and $e$, and then composing them together. In particular, there exists an algorithm that computes the epi-mono factorization such that the resulting matching $\mu(f)$ is that of \cite{GST2025} (\cref{C!SS:P-matching}).% Along the way, we also derive a simple algorithm for computing the barcode of the image of a morphism of persistence modules.

\section[Morse theory of Euclidean distance functions]{\citefield{GLRS2025}{title}}

In this paper we generalize Morse theory to distance functions $f \coloneq \distYX$ from a subset $Y \subseteq \R^n$ restricted to a subset $X \subseteq \R^n$. We show that, under certain conditions, the distance function $f$ is a continuous selection:

\begin{proposition}[{\cref{D!P:regular-value}}]
  Suppose that $Y$ is smooth and let $x \in \R^n$ be a regular value of the exponential map $\varphi \colon NY \to \R^n$, where $NY$ denotes the normal bundle of $Y$\footnote{At every point $y$ in $Y$, the normal space $N_y Y$ is the orthogonal space to the tangent space $T_y Y$.}. Then
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item
      $B(x, \dist_Y(x)) \cap Y$ is a finite set $\{y_0, \ldots, y_k\}$,
    \end{enumerate}
    and for all $\varepsilon > 0$ small enough, there exists $\delta >0$ such that
    \begin{enumerate}[resume]
    \item
      for every $i \in \{0, \ldots, k\}$, the function $f_i \coloneq \dist_{B(y_i, \varepsilon) \cap Y}$ is $\mathcal{C}^2$ on $B(x, \delta)$;
    \item
      the function $\dist_Y\big|_{B(x, \delta)}$ is a continuous selection of the $f_i$: specifically, $\dist_Y\big|_{B(x, \delta)} = \min_i f_i$.
    \end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

We can therefore apply the results of \cite{APS1997} to distance functions $\distYX$ where all critical points are regular points of the exponential map on $Y$. Recall from \cref{!D:continuous-selection} that a nondegenerate critical point $x$ of a continuous selection has two indices, a piecewise linear index $k(x)$ and a quadratic index $\iota(x)$. Given a pair $(X, Y)$ of subsets of $\R^n$ such that $\distYX$ only has nondegenerate critical points, we denote by $C_{k, \iota}(X, Y)$ the critical points with indices $(k, \iota)$. We recover the Morse lemmas and inequalities presented in \cref{S:Morse-theory}. Moreover, we can exchange the roles of $X$ and $Y$: if $\dist_X\big|_Y$ also only has nondegenerate critical points, then combining the resulting Morse inequalities gives the following equality relating the Euler characteristics of $X$ and $Y$ and the number of critical points (\cref{D!coro:duality}):
\[
  \chi(Y) + \sum_{k, \iota \geq 0} (-1)^{k + \iota} \#C_{k, \iota}(X, Y) = \chi(X) + \sum_{k, \iota \geq 0} (-1)^{k + \iota} \#C_{k, \iota}(Y, X).
\]

In the second half of the paper, we study the case where $X$ and $Y$ are generic algebraic hypersurfaces, i.e., zero sets of polynomial functions. By \index{generic property}\textbf{generic} we mean that the results hold for a dense open subset of polynomials. We summarize our results as follows:

\begin{theorem}
  Let $p \in \cc{P}_{d_1}$ and $q \in \cc{P}_{d_2}$ be generic polynomials, and let $X = Z(p)$ and $Y = Z(q)$ be their zero sets. Then, depending on the values of $d_1 = \deg(X)$ and $d_2 = \deg(Y)$ the following is true:
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item if $d_1 \geq 3$ and $d_2 \geq 4$, then the function $\dist$ is a continuous selection near each of its critical points (\cref{D!C:C2selection});
    \item if $d_1, d_2 \geq 4$, then every critical point of $\dist$ not in $Y$ is nondegenerate (\cref{D!T:genericity-nondegeneracy});
    \item if $d_1, d_2 \geq 3$, then for every $k \in \{1, \ldots, n\}$, the number of $k$-critical points of $\distYX$ can be bounded by
    \[
      \#C_{k, *}(X, Y) \leq c(k, n) \deg(X)^n \deg(Y)^{n (k + 1)},
    \]
    for some number $c(k, n) > 0$ depending on $n$ and $k$ only (\cref{D!T:finite-crit-pt}).
  \end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

We get analogous results when $X = \R^n$ (\cref{D!R:X-is-Rn-case}).
